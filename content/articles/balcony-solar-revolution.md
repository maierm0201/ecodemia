A panel on the balcony railing, a plug in the socket, done: hardly any climate technology has a lower barrier to entry than the plug-in solar kit. That is precisely what makes it disruptive.

## From Niche Product to Hardware-Store Staple

For years, registration bureaucracy and technical requirements held the segment back. Today, a simple online registration suffices in many places, and complete kits sit on hardware-store shelves. Prices have fallen far enough that a set often pays for itself within a few years.

## Why the Small Systems Matter

A single balcony unit covers only part of a household's base load — router, fridge, standby devices. But scale changes everything:

- **Millions of stakeholders:** People who generate their own power look differently at electricity prices and grid politics.
- **Renters included:** For the first time, the energy transition is open to people without a house of their own.
- **Visibility:** Every panel on a railing is a billboard for the neighbourhood.

> "Balcony solar is the gateway drug of the energy transition," jokes one energy adviser. "Once you watch your own generation in an app, you want more."

## The Next Stage

Small batteries, smart plugs and dynamic tariffs are turning the mini-systems into building blocks of a flexible grid. The revolution is not coming from the mega power plant — it is hanging on the railing.
