For decades, the rule was simple: build tall, build with concrete and steel. Cross-laminated timber — solid wood panels glued in alternating layers — has ended that certainty. Multi-storey timber buildings have gone from experiment to standard project.

## Why Wood Matters for the Climate

Cement and steel production are among the world's largest industrial emission sources. Wood flips the equation: the tree removes CO₂ from the atmosphere, and inside a building that carbon stays locked up for decades.

- **Lighter:** Timber structures weigh less and need smaller foundations.
- **Faster:** Prefabricated modules cut construction times dramatically.
- **Healthier:** Visible wood measurably improves how occupants perceive indoor climate.

## The Fire Question — Settled, but Sticky

Massive timber chars on the outside and thereby protects its core; modern timber buildings meet the same fire codes as conventional ones. Building authorities in many countries have modernised their regulations accordingly — the prejudices are outliving the paragraphs.

> "We have built with wood for a thousand years. The only new thing is that we are taking it seriously again," says one architect.

## The Limit of the Boom

Sourcing is decisive: only wood from verifiably sustainable forestry makes the climate math work. Certification, regional supply chains and cascade use — structural timber first, materials second, energy last — separate real progress from green paint.
