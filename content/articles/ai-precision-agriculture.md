A robot rolls at walking pace across a beet field. Its cameras distinguish crop from weed in milliseconds; a precise hoeing mechanism removes only the latter. No herbicide, no blanket spraying — weed control plant by plant.

## From Watering Can to Single Treatment

Precision agriculture replaces blanket measures with targeted ones:

- **Spot spraying:** Nozzles open only above identified problem plants, saving the bulk of the chemicals.
- **Sensor-guided fertilisation:** Nitrogen goes where measurements show demand — not where the schedule says.
- **Data-driven irrigation:** Soil moisture sensors end watering by gut feeling.

## What It Means for the Environment

Less pesticide means less runoff into waterways and more room for insects at the field margin. Precise fertilisation cuts nitrous-oxide emissions and nitrate in groundwater.

> "The technology finally lets us work as precisely as we always wanted to," says a farmer who has run hoeing robots for three years.

## The Open Questions

Who owns the field data? What happens when the manufacturer switches off the server? And can small farms afford the systems, or will the technology accelerate consolidation? The answers will decide whether digital agriculture becomes sustainable ecologically AND socially.
