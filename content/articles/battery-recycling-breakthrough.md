Electric mobility made a promise: clean transport without new dependencies. Whether it keeps that promise is decided partly at the end of the battery's life — in the recycling plant.

## From Shredding to Chemistry

Early processes melted batteries down and lost most of the lithium along the way. Modern plants work differently: disassembly under protective atmosphere, mechanical separation into "black mass", then hydrometallurgical recovery of battery-grade metals.

- **Recovery rates** above 90 percent for nickel, cobalt and copper are state of the art.
- **Lithium**, long the problem child, now reaches high recovery rates as well.
- **Second life:** Many packs spend years as stationary storage before recycling at all.

## Why It Matters Geopolitically

Every recycled tonne of battery metal reduces import dependency and pressure on mining regions.

> "We have already imported the raw materials. The only question is whether we circulate them or throw them on the landfill," says one circular-economy researcher.

## The Bottleneck Is Collection

The technology works — now it needs feedstock. Return logistics, clear responsibilities and recycling quotas will determine how fast the pilot becomes an industry. The raw material of the future is already parked in our garages.
