  ## Was der Garten der Stadt zurückgibt

  Studien zu urbanen Gärten zeigen ein wiederkehrendes Muster:

  - **Kühlung:** Bewachsene, bewässerte Flächen bleiben an Hitzetagen deutlich kühler als versiegelte.

  Der vielleicht wichtigste Effekt lässt sich schwer in Zahlen fassen: Gemeinschaftsgärten schaffen Begegnung über Milieus hinweg.

  ## Ehrlich bleiben

  Urbane Gärten ersetzen keine Landwirtschaft, und ihr direkter CO₂-Effekt ist bescheiden. Ihr Wert liegt woanders: Sie machen Städte hitzefester, lebendiger und lehren eine Generation, woher Essen kommt. Das ist keine Nebensache – es ist Infrastruktur.

Where a parking lot once stood, chard, tomatoes and marigolds now grow. Scenes like this exist in almost every large European city today — and they are more than an idyll.

## What the Garden Gives Back to the City

Research on urban gardens shows a recurring pattern:

- **Cooling:** Planted, watered plots stay significantly cooler on hot days than sealed surfaces.
- **Biodiversity:** Wild bees and butterflies find more food in diverse beds than in some farmland.
- **Water:** Open soil absorbs cloudbursts where asphalt fires them straight into the sewers.
- **Knowledge:** Children who grow carrots understand food differently from those who only know it shrink-wrapped.

## The Social Harvest

The most important effect may be the hardest to quantify: community gardens create encounters across social milieus.

> "People talk to each other here who would otherwise never meet. The kohlrabi is just the excuse," says one garden coordinator.

## Staying Honest

Urban gardens will not replace agriculture, and their direct carbon effect is modest. Their value lies elsewhere: they make cities more heat-resilient, more alive, and they teach a generation where food comes from. That is not a side issue — it is infrastructure.
