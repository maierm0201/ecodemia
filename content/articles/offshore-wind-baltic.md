For wind planners, the Baltic Sea is a gift: shallow depths, steady winds, short distances to shore and almost no tidal range. Small wonder the bordering states have agreed on a massive build-out.

## From Pledge to Plug

Between a political commitment and electricity in the grid lie years — and a chain of bottlenecks:

- **Grid connection:** Subsea cables and converter platforms are booked out years in advance.
- **Installation vessels:** The specialised ships needed for ever-larger turbines are scarce worldwide.
- **Ports:** Heavy-load quays and storage for 100-metre blades are missing in many places.
- **Skilled labour:** From welders to service technicians, the industry competes for the same people.

## Cooperation Over Parochialism

The most interesting trend is cross-border: hybrid projects in which a wind farm simultaneously connects two countries and doubles as a transmission corridor between them.

> "Every kilowatt-hour from the Baltic is valuable twice over — as clean power and as independence," says one energy economist.

## What to Watch Next

The auction rounds of the coming years will show whether the cost reductions of the past decade can hold. If the build-out succeeds, the Baltic will move from sideshow to one of Europe's most important power plants.
